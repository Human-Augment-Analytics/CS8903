
## What Was Presented

Walked through the second version of the researcher guide, built based on Bri's prior feedback to be more direct and detailed. Covered:

- What a unit meeting is
- The role of faculty — faculty do not run unit meetings, they observe, give notes, and help with blockers. This was intentional per Bri's feedback
- Project representative model — each project sends one rep, responsible for representing the team and presenting a weekly update
- Slide template (PowerPoint) — non-attending members fill it out, covers what they worked on, what it means for the project, what they're working on next, and blockers or questions
- Placeholder for what happens if a member doesn't send their slide
- Working sessions — self-organized, required at least once per semester (number can change based on what Bri wants)
- Recording instructions — using an LLM to generate a transcript summary and post to time-log
- Enforcement and grading — the most important section given past failed attempts. Requirements are graded, not extra credit, since extra credit did not work previously. Exact percentage is up to faculty or admin to decide
- Weekly reporting via bot — still in progress, reached out to relevant people for direction

## Project Channel Audit Update

Completed a second look at project channels, this time focused specifically on what did or did not work per Bri's request. Finding: successful collaboration in every case traced back to one person in the group taking initiative — sending reminders, collecting reports, keeping things moving. This informed the decision to split responsibility across multiple team members in the guide rather than relying on one person, so the whole group does not fail if one person drops off.
