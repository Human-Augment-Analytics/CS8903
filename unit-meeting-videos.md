# Video Tutorial Requirements
## HAAG Unit Meetings Initiative

---

## Video 1 — Researcher-Facing

**Audience:** All HAAG researchers, especially new members who don't know what a unit is

**Goal:** Explain what a unit meeting is and exactly what's required of them, so nobody has an excuse to say "I didn't know."

**Must cover:**

1. **What is a unit** — a group of projects with a similar research topic, meets bi-weekly, separate from individual project meetings

2. **The designated rep model**
   - Each team designates one person to represent them at meetings
   - Anyone on the team can attend if they want — not limited to just the rep
   - If the rep can't attend, the rep finds a substitute from the team

3. **If you're not attending, here's what you owe:**
   - Fill out the slide template (link to the PowerPoint) and send it to your rep by **5pm the day before the meeting**
   - Include speaker notes on the slide so the rep can present it accurately
   - Submit your weekly report to both your project channel and your unit channel every Friday

4. **The LLM shortcut** — show how to upload your weekly report + the template to Claude/ChatGPT and auto-fill the slide in under a minute

5. **After the meeting**
   - Everyone must watch the recording, whether they attended or not
   - If your work is discussed and there's a follow-up question, you're required to respond in the recording thread

6. **Grading** — this is not optional or extra credit. Missing slides, missing reports, and not responding to bot reminders all affect your participation grade. Show the actual bot reminder they'll see in Slack.

**Tone:** Direct and practical — like a "here's exactly what to do" walkthrough, not a sales pitch

**Length target:** 3–5 minutes

## Video 2 — Faculty-Facing

**Audience:** Faculty advisors and computational advisors, both current and being recruited to lead a new unit

**Goal:** Reassure them this new structure removes work from them, not adds to it, and set clear expectations for their role.

**Must cover:**

1. **Why this changed** — briefly, without dwelling on it: unit meetings were inconsistent and unstructured in the past, leading to faculty burnout, which is part of why some units stopped meeting

2. **Your role is now very narrow:**
   - Show up
   - Give advice and feedback
   - That's it — you should walk into a meeting and find everything already prepared

3. **What researchers now handle on their own:**
   - Agenda posted before every meeting
   - Slides collected and prepared by the rep
   - Post-meeting reports and recordings
   - All grading and enforcement — you don't have to track attendance or participation yourself

4. **Flexibility built in**
   - Not every unit will have a faculty advisor at all times — some may start without one and get advertised to faculty later
   - A computational advisor can lead a unit if no faculty is available
   - Meetings still happen either way — this system doesn't depend on you being available every time

5. **If you're asked to join a new unit** — briefly explain what that process looks like (this can be a placeholder for now since the "how units get created" process is still being defined — Minkyung and Arjun can help flag open questions here)

**Tone:** Reassuring and low-commitment — the message is "this makes your job easier," not "here's a new obligation"

**Length target:** 2–3 minutes

---

## Shared Requirements for Both Videos

- Reference the actual guide document so viewers know where to find full details
- Keep production simple — screen recording with voiceover is fine, doesn't need to be polished
- End each video with a way to give feedback (e.g., "reply in this thread" or a short form)
- These videos are also a feedback-gathering tool — after sharing them, collect reactions from a few faculty and a few researchers before wider rollout
